## 0 — First, check whether the 22 August requests went out

[OUTREACH-2026-08-22](OUTREACH-2026-08-22.md) drafted two emails. The first asks
the BMD-45 authors at IISc for **short continuous clips** from the Bengaluru
Safe City cameras that BMD-45 was sampled from. Nothing in the repository
records whether either email was sent or answered.

That first email matters more than everything below. It is the only route found
so far to footage that is Indian, fixed, elevated, continuous and licensed in a
single step — the headline protocol in
[ADR-025](decisions/ADR-025-headline-folds-are-real-india.md).

**Status as of 2026-10-15: treated as an unsent draft.** Neither the repository
nor this session can see the project owner's mailbox. Check the Sent folder and
the inbox, then use exactly one of the two versions below — never both. Record
which one went, and its date, in this file.

**Why the ask is narrower than BMD-45's licence.** BMD-45's CC BY 4.0 covers the
published *stills*. It grants nothing about the continuous recordings they were
sampled from, which belong to the Safe City programme. So the request asks for
each intended use to be confirmed **in writing**, rather than assuming the
stills' licence carries over to footage.

**What BMD-45's own README now tells us (2026-10-15).** The images were
sampled for temporal diversity "from long-duration CCTV recordings" from about
3,679 Safe City cameras operated by Bengaluru Police, recorded in February
2025, 06:00–18:00. The metadata holds no camera ids and no per-image times, so
the published release cannot be turned into sequences (DATASETS.md). The
recordings exist; the ask is for a few of them. Naming them precisely is what
makes the request easy to answer.

## 1 — City of Bellevue, Traffic Video Dataset

**Why.** Bellevue is the only candidate found with long continuous video from
fixed, elevated cameras that frame a whole junction: about 101 hours from five
cameras. Its README says it is "made available for research purposes", and the
repository has no licence file. The gate in `scripts/check_sources.py` requires
a licence that permits the intended use, and "research purposes" does not say
whether derived data can be published.

**Where to send it.** The dataset is published from the City of Bellevue's
GitHub organisation (`github.com/City-of-Bellevue/TrafficVideoDataset`), which
lists no contact address. Two routes, in order: an issue on that repository,
and the City of Bellevue Transportation Department's public contact. Do not
guess an email address.

> **Subject:** Bellevue Traffic Video Dataset — terms of use for a university
> research project
>
> Dear City of Bellevue Transportation team,
>
> I am a final-year B.Tech Computer Science (ML/AI) student at [university],
> working on short-horizon traffic congestion forecasting from fixed traffic
> cameras. We would like to use the Bellevue Traffic Video Dataset
> (github.com/City-of-Bellevue/TrafficVideoDataset) in this work, and I could
> not find licence terms beyond the README's note that it is available for
> research purposes.
>
> Could you confirm whether the following uses are permitted?
>
> 1. Using the videos to train and evaluate a forecasting model in an academic
>    project and a resulting conference paper.
> 2. Publishing **derived** data only: per-lane vehicle counts, congestion
>    labels, and trained model weights. We would not redistribute the video.
> 3. Including a small number of still frames in the paper, to illustrate a
>    camera's viewpoint.
>
> We would cite the dataset and the City of Bellevue in any publication, and
> follow whatever attribution wording you prefer.
>
> Thank you for releasing this footage.
>
> [name], [university], [project supervisor]
