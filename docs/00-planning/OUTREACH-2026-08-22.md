# Outreach to send today — two emails, batched

**Send before the guide's 29 August decision, not after.** Replies take days. A
mail sent today can inform the decision; a mail sent after it can only reopen
it. Neither of these competes with placement preparation — they are twenty
minutes and then waiting.

Both asks are the same underlying request from two directions: **continuous,
elevated, Indian traffic footage with a licence we can build on.** That is the
one input that fixes [P58](PRD-CHANGELOG.md)'s domain problem and
[P68](PRD-CHANGELOG.md)'s licence problem in a single move, and it is the only
route that does not cost a capture campaign.

## 1 — IISc AIM, the BMD-45 authors

**Why this is the highest-value hour on the board.** BMD-45 is on-domain in
every respect that matters: Bengaluru Safe City CCTV, elevated fixed viewpoint,
mixed Indian traffic, CC BY 4.0, faces already blurred, already cited in this
project. The only thing that makes it unusable for us is that it ships **~12.5
frames per camera** — sampled deliberately for spatial and temporal diversity —
where a forecasting window needs **90 consecutive observations**.

**The underlying footage is continuous, and somebody has it.** Even a handful of
cameras' worth of continuous clips would give us an on-domain, fold-eligible
corpus with clean provenance: no capture, no institutional permission chain, no
blurring pipeline, no DPDP exposure.

> **Subject:** BMD-45 — request for short continuous clips for a traffic
> forecasting benchmark
>
> Dear Prof. / Dr. [name],
>
> I am a final-year B.Tech CSE (ML/AI) student at [university], working on
> camera-only short-horizon traffic congestion forecasting for unstructured
> Indian traffic. We use BMD-45 in our detection work and it is the strongest
> Indian elevated-CCTV dataset we have found — thank you for releasing it under
> CC BY 4.0.
>
> Our forecasting task needs *sequences* rather than isolated frames: 60
> observations at 2-second spacing plus a 60-second horizon, so about 3 minutes
> of continuous footage per training window. BMD-45's frames are sampled for
> diversity, which is right for detection and means a single window cannot be
> formed from them.
>
> Would it be possible to release, or share for research use, **short continuous
> clips — 15 to 20 minutes each from a small number of cameras** — from the
> recordings BMD-45 was sampled from? Even five or six cameras would let us
> build an evaluation set that is genuinely Indian; our current corpus is
> Bangladeshi and British footage, which we do not think can carry an
> Indian-traffic claim.
>
> We would cite BMD-45 as the source, release any derived annotations under CC
> BY 4.0, and are happy to work under whatever anonymisation or access
> conditions the Safe City agreement requires.
>
> If continuous footage cannot be shared, we would be grateful for any pointer
> to continuous elevated Indian traffic video that can be.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [name]

**Enclose:** the one-line description of the window arithmetic (T=60, 2 s,
60 s horizon, 178 s minimum). It makes the ask concrete and shows we are not
asking for the whole archive.

## What each reply changes

| reply | consequence |
|---|---|
| BMD-45 clips available | the domain **and** licence problems close together; capture becomes unnecessary; the paper keeps its Indian claim |
| BMD-45 no, but a pointer | evaluate that source against the same gates before committing to capture |
| TrafficCAM licence permissive | `e_rickshaw` becomes publishable; the detector's class coverage claim survives |
| TrafficCAM licence restrictive or no reply | `e_rickshaw` must be re-annotated from a releasable source, or dropped from the claimed taxonomy |
| both silent by 29 Aug | the guide decides on the corpus we have, and the answer is the "change the claim" branch |
