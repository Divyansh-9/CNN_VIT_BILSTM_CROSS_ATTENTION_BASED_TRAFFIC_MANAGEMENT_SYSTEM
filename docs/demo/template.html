What the camera sees

A fixed camera on a footbridge over Rampura, Dhaka. This is the viewpoint the system is designed for: mounted high, pointed down at the road, never moving. The same camera watches the same junction all day.

It is an ordinary, difficult scene — a tree blocks the left third, motorcycles and auto-rickshaws mix with cars, and people walk between vehicles. Nothing was cleaned up for this page.

2

Finding the vehicles

The first model draws a box around every vehicle and names what it is. It was trained on Indian and Bangladeshi road imagery, so it knows classes that a general-purpose model does not — auto-rickshaw, e-rickshaw, and cattle among them.

That distinction turns out to matter more than it sounds. It is revisited at step 4.

3

Sorting vehicles onto approaches

A junction has several arms, and a signal controls them separately. So a single total is not useful — the system needs to know how many vehicles are waiting on each approach.

Two regions are drawn once for this camera. Every vehicle is assigned to whichever region its centre falls inside. Vehicles too far away to belong to either are counted as unassigned and discarded rather than guessed at.

The measuring instrument changed the answer

Judged with the off-the-shelf model, this junction looks almost static — it would have suggested the whole forecasting problem was too easy to be worth solving. Judged with the right detector, the same twenty minutes has four times as much movement between states.

Same road, same footage, opposite conclusions. This is why the detector had to be built before anything else could be trusted.

5

Turning counts into a reading

A number of vehicles is not yet a congestion level. The project's specification sets fixed cut-offs: under 5 vehicles is low, 5 to 15 is medium, above 15 is high.

Run against this camera, those cut-offs break. The quietest moment in twenty minutes still had 9 vehicles in view, so the low band never once occurred — the red line on the chart above is never reached.

Why this is a finding, not a bug

"Five vehicles" means something different through a wide elevated lens than through a narrow one. A count threshold describes the view, not the road, so a single set of numbers cannot be correct for every camera.

The fix is to calibrate the cut-offs per camera and record them alongside the footage. On this camera the measured split is roughly low < 11 · medium 11–14 · high > 14. That change needs sign-off before it is adopted, because it alters a graded specification.

6

The bar any model has to clear

The system's actual job is prediction: given the last five minutes, say what the congestion level will be 60 seconds from now.

Before building anything clever, it is worth asking how well you do by guessing that nothing changes. On this footage that guess is right 69% of the time, because traffic is sluggish and the next minute usually resembles this one.

Two numbers that decide whether the project is worth doing

passes  The reading changes over the next minute in 31% of cases. Had it been near zero, nothing could ever have outperformed "assume no change", and there would be no problem left to solve.

open  That also sets a hard floor. Any model that scores below 69% is worse than assuming nothing happens, and reporting it as a success would be dishonest. This number goes in the paper next to every result.

What this page does and does not show

  • Real, end to end. Detection, approach assignment, counting, and both measurements above were produced by the committed code on the footage shown. The images are direct output, not mock-ups.
  • The forecasting model is not in these pictures. It is built and its training harness runs, but it has not yet been trained on this camera — so no prediction accuracy is claimed anywhere here.
  • The signal controller runs in simulation only. Results for it come from SUMO, a traffic simulator, and are reported separately. No claim on this page depends on it.
  • One camera. Every number describes this junction. Other cameras need their own regions drawn and their own cut-offs measured.
